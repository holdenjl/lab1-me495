# What the exemplars do well, and what changed

The first slide of each supplied PPTX is the poster reference. Remaining slides
were inspected for context; their experimental values are not used in our work.

| Feature | Exemplar 1 | Exemplar 2 | Change to our poster |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reading order | Three clear columns: methods, evidence, recommendations | Three columns with a visible process flow | Retained three columns; headings now state the problem, model evidence and physical alternatives |
| Visual hierarchy | White background, dark section bars, aligned plots | Consistent section bars and several figure groups | Replaced the large dark title block with a white title area; retained a narrow gold result strip |
| Model validation | Measurement/model overlays and a comparison table make accuracy inspectable | A simple measured-versus-model comparison avoids unnecessary curves | Removed the rigid-shaft line; put measured and predicted frequency/peak gain directly under the two-curve plot |
| Link to the task | Transient evidence connects the model to motor switching | Workflow explains how experimental parameters lead to solutions | Added the preferred physical design's switching response to the center column |
| Recommendations | Numbered recommendations connect to four plots | Names dimensions/material changes instead of only model parameters | Replaced abstract parameter targets with a resistor, two wheel/shaft combinations and a dimensioned oil-damper concept |
| Supporting visuals | Apparatus photograph and schematic explain the setup | Annotated rig and multiple characterization figures | Kept red boxes with instructions for the team's real photo and dimensioned CAD views |

The previous version's strengths were traceable calculations, consistent colors,
and clear separation of data from simulations. Its main weaknesses were abstract
damping/motor targets, a dense handout, and a rigid-shaft curve that distracted
from the requested flexible model. The revised poster gives the physical change,
predicted benefit and tradeoff together. The handout uses 12-point body text,
three pages, short explanations and build instructions; the full mathematics
stays in MATLAB.

Features we did not copy: exemplar-specific numerical results; percentages
calculated directly from dB; draft placeholders and clipped text in exemplar 2;
or claims that moving a resonance alone reduces its height. Our four design plots
track the new peaks and shade frequencies above the measured range. The third
design is explicitly conditional on a wider validation sweep.

The course poster guidance is reflected in the 16:9 landscape format, aligned
visual groups, limited branding, concise captions, recommendation near the top,
and a poster thumbnail in the separate three-page handout. Red placeholders are
deliberate, following the team's request; no apparatus images were generated.
