Reducing flexible-shaft vibration
1 / What we investigated
The Gulo 3000 task asks us to explain vibration when the motor switches on or off and identify four
passive changes that reduce it by at least 30%. We used the supplied bench measurements to build
and check a model, then compared physical modifications. No modified hardware has been tested.

The main finding
The measurements show a resonance near 5.65 Hz: repeated voltage changes near this frequency
produce unusually large speed oscillations. The flexible-shaft model reproduces this peak near 5.69
Hz. This supports shaft twisting as a cause of the bench vibration; it does not establish passenger
comfort in the vehicle.
Recommended first prototype
Replace only the distal flywheel with a 165 mm diameter steel wheel, and use a 4.50 mm diameter
shaft of the original material. Keep wheel thickness at 12.7 mm and shaft free length at 305 mm. The
model predicts a 44% lower resonance peak and 39% less switching motion, while preserving steady
speed at the same voltage.
How the evidence was collected
Three sweeps covered 4–10 Hz. Another 28 recordings captured steady motor operation, free
vibration and coastdown. Steady runs identified motor resistance and the relationship between speed
and generated voltage. Decay tests estimated energy losses. Only effective shaft stiffness was
adjusted to improve the sweep fit.
For the motor-disconnected coastdowns, the motor was physically uncoupled and both flywheels
remained connected by the shaft. Their combined inertia was therefore used in the calculations.
Duplicate copies of the data were not counted as extra tests.
